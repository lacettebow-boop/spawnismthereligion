Origins and development

History

There is no single neutral historical record for Spawnism in this archive. The timeline below records what community sources say about the belief's development and keeps those accounts separate from broader descriptions of reincarnation and rebirth.

Source note: The main historical account currently documented here comes from the community-edited TrueSpawnism Wiki. Its account should be read as a community source rather than as independently verified academic history.
☾ ───── ✦ ───── ☽

c. 2013 — The respawn-point inspiration

The TrueSpawnism Wiki says Spawnism was originally inspired sometime around 2013 by the respawn-point symbol in Roblox. According to that account, the game's mechanic of appearing again after death became a metaphor for resurrection, grace, and another chance to begin.

The same source presents this digital origin as the starting point for a belief about existence rather than merely a game reference. It says the idea of being "here again" developed into a personal spiritual framework centered on rebirth and second chances.

From symbol to spiritual framework

According to the TrueSpawnism Wiki, the early respawn metaphor was eventually understood as something spiritually meaningful by people who adopted the belief. The wiki describes the Spawn as a divine force for some followers, while noting that others interpret the Spawn as a spiritual force, creature, symbol, or something internal to themselves.

This source therefore describes Spawnism as developing through personal interpretation rather than from one universally fixed doctrine. That distinction matters when reading later community-created branches, rituals, and texts.

Development of branches

As the community developed, different interpretations of the Spawn were recorded as branches. The TrueSpawnism Wiki's branches page lists interpretations including Divine, Self-Spawn, Moon, Grateful, Angelic, Ecliptian, Solrynism, Divine Cycles, and others.

The branch list itself demonstrates that later Spawnist communities did not necessarily interpret the Spawn in exactly the same way. Some branches emphasize the nature of the Spawn, some focus on particular symbols or ideas, and some develop their own approaches to rebirth, ethics, or worship.

Texts and community documentation

Community-created texts appeared alongside these interpretations. The TrueSpawnism Wiki's page on the Book of Spawnism describes several works intended to collect or explain Spawnist beliefs, including an unfinished "Book of Spawn" / "Points of Spawn" text and other explanatory documents.

That page also makes clear that these texts do not necessarily function as a single required scripture for every Spawnist. It describes the book as something followers may use or build beliefs around while emphasizing that Spawnism is not tied to one text alone.

Community observances

Later community documentation also records holidays and rituals. The Holidays and Rituals page lists observances such as Exsomnis, Somnus, Devotee's Week, New Spawn Year, and Veilfall. The page itself says these celebrations are optional rather than mandatory.

Because these observances come from community-edited material and can include contributions from particular users, this archive treats them as documented community practices, not proof of a universal historical tradition dating back to the origin of Spawnism.

What this history can—and cannot—establish

The currently documented account gives a reported origin around 2013 and describes a later development from a respawn-point metaphor into a broader spiritual framework with multiple interpretations. It does not provide enough independent historical evidence to establish a complete chronology of every Spawnist community, branch, or individual belief.

For that reason, dates, authorship, branch formation, and later traditions on this page should be read with their stated source and scope in mind.

Primary community references: TrueSpawnism Wiki, The Branches, The Book of Spawnism and Spawnism “Bible”, and Holidays and Rituals.
